# An Idea That Waited a Decade for Its Moment

## 2014: When the Vision Was Ahead of Its Time

Long before generative AI entered the courtroom or the boardroom, a European consortium asked what would happen if the legislation and case law of an entire continent became openly addressable, interconnected and readable by machines.

Co-funded by the **European Commission (DG Justice)** and incubated by **ODINE** and the **Open Data Institute (London)**—together with the London School of Economics (LSE), the University of Amsterdam, the University of Sussex and FH Salzburg—**openlaws.eu** pioneered the concept of **Big Open Legal Data (BOLD)**.

At the time, the legal data standards were taking shape, but the world lacked the reasoning engines to bring them to life in daily legal practice.

## 2026: The Missing Counterpart

Today, the situation has inverted. Capable language models are everywhere—and fast becoming interchangeable commodities. Yet without verifiable legal roots, provenance and clear human boundaries, artificial fluency becomes a liability.

What began in 2014 as an open-data pioneer returns in 2026 as a **Legal Knowledge Factory**: calm, model-independent and built so legal organizations keep sovereignty over their knowledge.
